Who we are

A company built by people who got tired of stitching things together.

Since 2019

From one frustrated engineer to 240 people across four hubs.

Stackly started in a shared office in Brooklyn after our founder spent a year building the same ledger, compliance stack and reconciliation pipeline at three different fintechs. The conclusion was unavoidable: the plumbing was the product, and nobody should have to build it twice.

Today, we process billions annually across 40+ markets. We're still the same company at heart — small teams, high ownership, obsessive about correctness — just with more people and better infrastructure.

Our history

Six years, in six chapters.

The moments that shaped how we build — and what we refuse to compromise on.

2019

Founded in Brooklyn

Three engineers, one shared desk, and a working prototype of the double-entry ledger that still powers Stackly today.

2020

First 100 customers

Launched the Payments API in private beta. Reached $10M in monthly processed volume within nine months of the first integration.

2021

Ledger v2 & Series A

Rebuilt the ledger as an event-sourced engine. Raised a $40M Series A to expand into Europe and hire the compliance team.

2022

Global expansion

Opened offices in London and Singapore. Crossed $1B in annual processed volume and launched the Compliance engine.

2023

Payouts & instant settlement

Shipped the Payouts product with 24/7 instant settlement. Added Dubai as our fourth global office.

2025

Where we are now

$48.6B processed annually, 240 people, four hubs, and a platform that powers thousands of products in 40+ markets.

What drives us

Vision, mission, and the line we won't cross.

Our vision

Financial infrastructure as invisible as electricity.

We want a world where moving money across borders, currencies and rails is as unremarkable as sending a text message. Where every developer, on every team, in every market, has the same access to the same primitives that the largest institutions take for granted.

Our mission

Make the correct way the easy way.

Every decision we make — from our API design to our audit trails — is guided by one question: does this make it easier for our customers to do the right thing? Correctness should never be the expensive option, and compliance should never be an afterthought bolted on at the end.
